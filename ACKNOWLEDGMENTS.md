# Acknowledgments

This collection exists because mathematicians and programmers chose to expose the programs behind their pictures.

Special thanks to **Bill Casselman** for *Mathematical Illustrations: A Manual of Geometry and PostScript*, the accompanying PostScript examples, PiScript, and decades of mathematical-graphics teaching material. Casselman's published material is the conceptual anchor for this repository; restricted material is linked rather than republished.

Thanks to **Nathan Reading** for publishing compact computational PostScript for Coxeter-plane and noncrossing-partition figures, and for explicitly treating PostScript as a programming language rather than merely an output format.

Thanks to **John R. Stembridge** for publishing Coxeter graph-paper PostScript together with the Maple program used to generate related arrangements.

Thanks to **D. R. Wilkins** for making the source of a modern electronic edition of Newton's *Quadrature of Curves* available, including the MetaPost programs for its figures.

Thanks to **Michael La Croix** for writing mathematical illustrations directly in PostScript and for leaving substantial PostScript drawing programs visible in his doctoral thesis as well as describing his illustration practice publicly.

Thanks to the mathematicians represented in Harvard's PostScript collection, including **Noam Elkies** and **Curtis T. McMullen**, and to Harvard Mathematics for keeping those old source examples reachable.

Thanks to **John D. Hobby** and **AT&T Bell Laboratories** for MetaPost. The historical MetaPost distribution was placed in the public domain; its examples are particularly valuable because they make mathematical constraints, paths, interpolation, transforms, and drawing operations explicit.

Thanks to **Sergey Slyusarev** for the modern MetaPost implementation of Oliver Byrne's geometric visual language in `byrne-latex` and `byrne-euclid`, released as free software/content.

Thanks to **Donald Knuth** for making source material for many mathematical illustrations available, and for MetaFont/TeX work that helped establish programmable typography and mathematical graphics as serious computational subjects.

Thanks to **Toby Thurston** for *Drawing with MetaPost*, a large collection of intentionally readable technical-drawing programs distributed under the Open Publication License.

Thanks to **Alan G. Isaac** for *PostScript Drawing for the Sciences* and its unusually useful map of the older PostScript ecosystem.

Thanks to **Harold Abelson, Gerald Jay Sussman, Julie Sussman**, the SICP course-material contributors, and MIT for publishing the higher-order curve/drawing exercises that show another unusually clear route from small geometric primitives to a programmable drawing language.

Thanks to **Jyh-Ming Lien** for the ACD2D polygon-decomposition source, **Michael Joshua Ryan** and contributors for xpost, and the **Asymptote** authors and maintainers for keeping programmable technical graphics alive as free software.

Thanks also to the authors and maintainers of the related G-code, plotter, CNC, and geometric-drawing projects indexed here. They make it possible to compare a mathematical path with a pen path, a laser path, and a physical machine toolpath without inventing artificial programming exercises.

No acknowledgment here implies endorsement of this repository, affiliation with it, or permission beyond the terms stated by the original source.
